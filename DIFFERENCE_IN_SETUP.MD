CRGC paper's setting (arXiv 2203.12646): semi-honest two-party computation. Generator A has a fixed input a; evaluator B evaluates the same garbled circuit with arbitrarily many of its own inputs b. Reusability means "one a, many b". PRGC adds OT-based Yao sections per evaluation to stop leakage of a. Security is simulation-based input privacy in 2PC.

Our setting differs in four ways:

Single player, and that player is the adversary. There's no 2PC at all. The puzzle solver is an adversarial evaluator, not a semi-honest partner. CRGC's proofs are semi-honest privacy proofs; they say nothing about an adversary whose explicit goal is to shortcut a time-lock. The TLP security game (sequentiality) is a different object from CRGC's privacy game.

The role mapping is inverted. CRGC reusability is over the evaluator's input (b varies, a fixed). We need reusability over the generator's input: every puzzle has fresh (x, m). That's the Goldwasser-style rGC notion ("many encodings under one garbled circuit"), which CRGC explicitly does not provide — its one-time pad on a is only secure for one fixed a ("A has to construct a new RGC if a changes", §2.1). The reviewer's original complaint was a symptom of exactly this: m was placed in the evaluator-input slot because that's the only slot CRGC lets vary.

We de-interacted the OT. Standard and defensible — as discussed, when the encoder owns all inputs, OT adds nothing. The rGC/SRE literature does the same. This one is fine.

Fixed-mask reuse with changing inputs (the leftover issue). Even in v3, the reusable section (the whole SHA chain, ~135k gates) uses one fixed wire_flip mask from PSetup, and each puzzle delivers x ⊕ mask. Each puzzle's encoding is individually uniform (since x is uniform), but across puzzles the solver learns x_i ⊕ x_j relations. CRGC's security theorem does not cover this — it assumes a is fixed. Mitigating facts: the time-lock doesn't require x to be hidden (knowing x doesn't remove the T sequential evaluations), and x_i ⊕ x_j doesn't yield any x_k. But this is our heuristic, not a cited result.

Where this leaves the paper: the honest framing is that PRGC-as-encoded is a heuristic instantiation of the randomized encoding that Construction 4.1 wants, sitting between CRGC's 2PC privacy model and the rGC definition the TLP formally requires. The paper already gestures at this with "weaker security guarantees but more practical" — but that sentence currently undersells which guarantees are weakened. For the revision I'd state explicitly: (a) the composition is single-player and non-interactive by design; (b) per-puzzle freshness of the Yao section is what hides m; (c) the reusable section's fixed-mask encoding of x is outside CRGC's proven model, with a short argument (or assumption) for why the x_i ⊕ x_j leakage doesn't affect time-lock security. That's a defensible position. Claiming "we use PRGC from [47]" without those caveats is not — it's the unorthodox part reviewers will keep hitting.
